# TRVLR product vision

Status: working draft for team review, 2026-11-13. TRVLR is a working name. This is a product hypothesis, not a description of an implemented app.

## Problem

Students studying abroad may want to explore beyond their host city but struggle to find compatible travel companions across programs and coordinate affordable trips. Plans can be spread across group chats and other tools, making it harder to find people with similar destinations, dates, interests and budgets and turn interest into a shared plan. The frequency and severity of these problems still need validation.

## Who it is for

Initially, students already studying abroad who want to meet peers beyond their existing friends or program and plan independent trips. A possible first community is students from several programs in one host city planning short trips. This segment, its size and willingness to adopt or pay are unproven.

## What it aims to do

Provide a mobile-friendly social experience where students can discover potential travel companions and coordinate a trip together. Help people communicate expectations about dates, destinations and budgets, with clear controls over their information. Connecting travelers may enable shared costs; it does not guarantee cheaper or safer travel.

The team's notes mention profiles, discovering people, private messages and group chats as ideas. The exact first-release features and the way they work remain to be tested and prioritized.

## Boundaries

- Prioritize student value and a small number of understandable workflows. Staff administration, program hubs, formal trip registration and emergency alerts are later possibilities, not current commitments.
- Do not represent the product as an official registry, emergency service, identity guarantee, or substitute for program rules.
- Consider privacy, consent, moderation and personal safety from the beginning; do not collect more sensitive information than a validated workflow requires.

## Outcomes and validation

Desired outcomes: students find relevant companions, coordinate a trip more easily, and feel comfortable with what they share. Interview students about their most recent trips and existing tools; test whether a prototype helps them find compatible people and organize plans. Measure relevant matches, completed trip plans, repeat use and reported trust against a defined pilot baseline. Targets, data collection and success thresholds are not yet agreed.

Open questions: What motivates adoption over group chats and existing travel apps? How will enough students in one place be reached? What trust controls are necessary? Who pays, if anyone? Which small workflow should be built first?
