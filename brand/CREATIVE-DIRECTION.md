# Creative direction and decision record

2026-11-14 · Consolidated from Lucky's icon design conversation. Lucky selected the current Option A linework and balanced earth gradient. Team/Maggie approval remains pending.

## Meaning and audience

A speech bubble is the globe, supported by a globe arm and pedestal. It connects two parts of the student-abroad idea: conversations in city communities and discovering/planning shared travel. The product direction emphasizes student perspectives, practical recommendations, limited budgets and connections across programs. This logo is not a claim that map, board or multi-user functionality has shipped.

The visual character should feel clean, simple, welcoming and immediately recognizable, with the economy of the Messages and Instagram icons supplied as references. Those references informed clarity, boldness and negative space, not their proprietary symbols or palettes. Keep the app name out of the mark; TRVLR remains a placeholder.

## Selected direction

- Option A from the eight-round refinement: outlined bubble, curved globe arm, short connectors and filled rounded pedestal.
- The original preferred pedestal was restored after later thin bars/pills were rejected.
- Primary: white mark over the balanced earthy gradient.
- Reverse: gradient-colored arm, filled bubble and pedestal over white.
- Gradient remains continuous across the entire reverse mark through one mask.
- Use the approved palette's deep teal, muted green and warm brown as stops. No white/sand endpoint.
- Component strokes are consistent within each component, with no tapering. Selected A uses a heavier bubble (30) than arm (22); it is not a single-weight icon.

## Exploration and rejected directions

Initial ideas included a plain monochrome outline, a gradient outline, continent silhouettes, and a destination-pin/route interpretation. The destination-pin concept was rejected. Continent versions were rejected as small, ugly and overly detailed; do not revive them by default.

Simply making every line thicker did not satisfy the request for simplicity. Removing the base also lost the intended globe stand. Later alternatives replaced the chosen pedestal with thin bars or pill shapes; those were rejected. The favored base has rounded shoulders and corners and remains filled.

Gradients that faded to sand/white washed out the lower corner. Strong brown bands and a large brown region were rejected. The selected blend keeps green through the center and introduces warmth toward the lower right. Green/terracotta colors outside the approved palette appeared in earlier studies but are not used in the current master.

## Review process and evidence

The final A selection followed eight sequential render/inspect/revise rounds for each of A/B/C, in gradient and reverse treatments, with 24/32/64 px comparison thumbnails. Work covered weights, bubble clearance, tail projection and connector continuity. The resulting SVGs are the source, not a specification reconstructed from screenshots. Earlier review boards remain in the local outputs archive; the current brand folder carries only the selected direction and its reference material.

The chat originally requested at least three app/icon design resources plus another advice source. References consulted in that design exploration:

- [Apple app icon guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/app-icons): legibility and recognition.
- [Google launcher icon codelab](https://codelabs.developers.google.com/design-android-launcher): simple artwork and platform formats.
- [Material icon principles](https://m1.material.io/style/icons.html): geometric consistency and optical balance.
- [Adobe minimalist logo guidance](https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/design/discover/minimalist-logo-design.html): restraint and negative space.

These references informed judgment; they do not constitute certification, endorsement or a platform-compliant export package.
